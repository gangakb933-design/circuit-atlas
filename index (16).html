Start Here

1. What is a circuit?

A circuit is a closed loop. Electricity leaves the battery, travels through the parts, and returns to the battery. If the loop is broken, nothing works.

2. Think of it like water

Voltage
Water pressure. It pushes the flow.
Current
Water flow. How much moves each second.
Resistance
A narrow pipe. It slows the flow down.

3. Ohm's law

Voltage = Current × Resistance. If you know two of these, you can find the third. Try it in Calculators.

4. Safety first

  • Use only batteries of 9 V or less, and low-voltage kits.
  • Never connect anything to a wall socket.
  • Always check your wiring with an adult or teacher before you connect power.
  • If a part gets hot or smells, disconnect the battery straight away.

Myths, Busted

  • "Batteries store electricity like a bucket." No. They store chemical energy that is turned into electrical energy.
  • "More voltage always makes an LED brighter." Without a resistor it burns out. The resistor controls the current.
  • "A resistor makes electricity disappear." It turns the energy into heat.
  • "The LED’s legs can go either way." The long leg must face the + side.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Connecting an LED with no resistor.
  • Putting the battery in backwards.
  • Joining + and − with one wire, which is a short circuit.
  • Placing parts across the breadboard’s middle gap.
  • Forgetting that all the GND wires must join together.

Breadboard Guide

The holes in each row of five are connected underneath. The long strips along the edges are the power rails, one for + and one for −. The middle gap splits the board, so chips usually sit straddling it.

Multimeter Guide

  • Voltage (V): Put the probes across the part. The circuit stays powered.
  • Current (A): The meter goes in series, so you must break the circuit. Get help before you do this.
  • Resistance (Ω): Turn the power off and take the part out of the circuit first.

Project Ideas

  • Beginner: LED flashlight, night light with an LDR, buzzer alarm.
  • Intermediate: Servo sweep with Arduino, distance beeper with HC-SR04.
  • Advanced: Temperature logger with DHT11, robot with DC motors.
